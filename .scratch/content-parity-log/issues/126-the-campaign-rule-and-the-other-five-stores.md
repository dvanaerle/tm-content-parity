**What to answer:** should `IMAGE_CAMPAIGN` learn the other five stores' campaign
vocabulary — `rabatt`, `angebot`, `aktion`, `réduction`, `promotion`, `discount`, `offre` —
or is a Dutch-only image pattern the right pattern?

The pattern is Dutch. ADR 0003 already refused a Dutch *text* anchor for exactly this
reason, and ticket 89 §6 measured what each candidate word drags in on the text side. Nobody
has asked the question on the image side, and 101's out-of-scope bullet is where it has been
sitting.

**This ticket exists to be answered, not assumed.** A first measurement, below, points at
"no". Confirm or overturn it; do not implement from it.

## Why the question exists

101 narrowed the pattern's boundaries and left its vocabulary alone. That leaves a stated
asymmetry: a campaign image called `zomeractie-2027.jpg` is caught in `nl` and `be` and an
identical German one called `sonderaktion-2027.jpg` is not. Either the asymmetry is a defect
or the Dutch-only pattern is correct for a reason worth writing down. Both are publishable
answers; the ticket is done when one of them is recorded.

## What to produce

- Every image key in the corpus matching each candidate word, per store, with what the file
  actually is — the page it sits on, its path and its `alt`. A count alone does not decide
  this; 101 was wrong for two days because nobody looked at the files.
- For each candidate word: its true positives (campaign artwork) and its false positives
  (everything else), and the **finding class each false positive holds today**. A word that
  hides a `work` finding costs more than one that hides a `diagnostic`.
- A recommendation: add all, add some, or add none — with the reason, and with the
  asymmetry above either accepted or fixed.

So on the only campaign artwork this project has ever recorded:

- `korting` and `actie` catch **4 of 4 filenames, in all six stores**.
- `rabatt`, `angebot`, `aktion`, `réduction`, `promotion`, `promo`, `discount`, `offre`,
  `offer` and `sonder` catch **0 of 4, in every store including their own**.

This is the answer, and it is the opposite shape to ADR 0003. ADR 0003 refused a Dutch
**text** anchor because the banner *copy* is translated —
`10% korting…` / `10% Rabatt…` / `10% discount…`,
`docs/adr/0003-regions-are-excluded-at-extraction.md:109-113` — and a Dutch word is
therefore blind in four stores. Ticket 89 §7 measured that objection and it held: 0 banner

written per store by a translator; the artwork is cut once by a designer and uploaded under
one Dutch name, exactly as `icon_offerte.png` is served to all six stores from
`/media/wysiwyg/tm/nl-nl/afbeeldingen/overig/`. The two surfaces have opposite language
behaviour, so ADR 0003's conclusion does not carry to the image side, and neither does its
premise. That is why this question was worth asking rather than inheriting.

### 4. Three corrections to `## First measurement`

The measurement is confirmed in its numbers and in its conclusion. Three of its statements
do not survive re-derivation.

**(a) `offer` under a letter boundary costs nothing, not six.** The measurement says
`offer`/`offre` "takes the quote icon in all six stores". That is true of a bare substring
arm and **false** of an arm written to 101's own convention.
`(?<![a-z])offers?(?![a-z])` does **not** match `icon_offerte.png`: `offer` is followed by
`t`, a letter. Tested directly against the key. `aktion` is **not** rescued the same way —
`(?<![a-z])aktions?(?![a-z])` still matches `aktions-update_erhalten.jpg`, because `-` is
a separator and not a letter. So the honest cost of a boundary-correct import is **1 work
finding, from `aktion` alone**, not seven. This does not change the recommendation: 1 work
finding hidden for 0 true positives is still a loss, and §1 shows the gain is 0 by
construction and not by accident. But the "seven" argues against a pattern nobody would
now write, and the ticket should not rest on it.

**(b) "After 101 all six stores agree" is a prediction, not a present fact.** Today the
`actie-updates` block does **not** agree across the stores: `nl`, `be` and `uk` carry
`image-campaign` (diagnostic, hidden — `compare/vocabulary.mjs:202-206`,
`web/src/components/Ledger.jsx:120`), and `de` carries `image-missing` (work). Verified in
the reports. The agreement arrives only when 101 lands. **What does hold pre-101** is the
other half of the claim: `aktions-update_erhalten.jpg` is `image-missing` **today**,
because the current pattern says `actie(?!f)` and `aktions` does not contain `actie`. So
"adding `aktion` would undo 101" is right in substance but conditional in tense — pre-101,
adding `aktion` would make all six agree in the **wrong** direction, hiding `de`'s work
finding to match the other five instead of the reverse.

**(c) The stated asymmetry dissolves after 101; it is not fixed by adding German.**
`## Why the question exists` says `zomeractie-2027.jpg` is caught in `nl` and `be` while
`sonderaktion-2027.jpg` is not caught in `de`. True **today**. After 101 the `actie` arm
is gone (`101-…md:126-130`, and its own trap at `:230-235` names this file), so
`zomeractie-2027.jpg` is **not** caught either, and both land as `image-missing` in every
store. 101 removes the asymmetry from the Dutch side. Adding German vocabulary would
remove it from the German side and would re-create, in five more languages, the sub-word
defect 101 exists to remove.

### 5. What each word would wrongly take, beyond today's corpus

A zero today is not a licence. Judged on the shape of the names this site actually uses:

- **`aktion`** is the worst of the eleven. The German analogue of `actievoorwaarden` is
  `aktionsbedingungen`, and it is a **terms** page, not a campaign — `de/carport`'s banner
  links to `self/aktionsbedingungen-10-rabatt-terrassenueberdachungen`
  (`data/probe-promo-banner.json`). An image named for that page would be hidden. The arm
  already takes `aktions-update`, boundary or not.
- **`angebot`** would take a German quote icon. The `de` `alt` of `icon_offerte.png` is
  literally `Angebot`; a German-named copy of that file is one upload away.
- **`offer` / `offre`** must survive `offerte`, Dutch for a quotation, which is in the
  corpus in all six stores. A letter-bounded arm survives it; a bare one does not, and the
  bare one is what the question asks for.
- **`promo`** is the loosest arm proposed: it is a prefix of `promotie` and `promotional`,
  and bounded it still takes any `promo-*.jpg`.
- **`reduction`** is an ordinary English noun (`noise-reduction`), and its accented twin
  `réduction` would match nothing anyway — uploaded filenames on this site are ASCII.
- **`rabatt`, `discount`, `sonder`** carry no collateral found here. They also carry no
  value: 0 of 4 on the artwork, in their own stores.

**89 §6's German numbers are text units, not filenames** — `aktionsbedingungen` 91,
`sonderaktionen` 9, `aktion` 6, `rabattaktion` 1, counted over production **content
units**. They are cited above only to name a page whose title an image might borrow. They
are not image evidence and are not counted as any word's true positives.

### 6. Recommendation

**Add none.** Four reasons, in the order that decides it.

1. **The words have no true positives even where they belong.** The campaign artwork in
   `de`, `fr`, `be_fr` and `uk` was named `kortingactie`. `korting` catches it; `rabatt`
   and `angebot` do not. The pattern is not Dutch by oversight — it is Dutch because the
   filenames are.
2. **Every measurable effect is loss.** 7 `work` findings hidden into `diagnostic` for an
   unbounded import, 1 for a boundary-correct one, against 0 gained. `image-campaign` is
   `diagnostic` and hidden behind the noise toggle, so each loss is invisible by
   construction — the mechanism that let 29 of 29 go unnoticed in 101.
3. **ADR 0003's reasoning does not transfer, and this is worth writing down.** ADR 0003
   refused a Dutch anchor because *copy* is translated. Filenames are not. The image side
   is the one surface where a Dutch anchor reaches all six stores, and §1 measured it. The
   ADR needs no amendment; this ticket records the boundary of its scope.
4. **`#campaign-banner` is the mechanism, and `IMAGE_CAMPAIGN` is a net.** ADR 0003, 89 §7
   and 101 all settle this. Widening the net with no fish is cost with no benefit; if
   artwork ever appears outside the banner it will be named `kortingactie`, as the last one
   was.

**The asymmetry is ACCEPTED**, on the correction in §4(c): it is a pre-101 artefact, 101
removes it by dropping the Dutch side, and after 101 `zomeractie-2027.jpg` and
`sonderaktion-2027.jpg` are treated identically — both `image-missing`, in every store.
101's trap at `:230-235` already records that trade-off. Nothing further is owed.

- **Independent of 101**: §1 (the artwork was Dutch-named in every store), §3's
  true-positive column, §5, and reasons 1, 3 and 4 above. These rest on the probe, the
  extractor and the ADRs, not on the pattern's current text.
- **Independent of 101, and already true**: `icon_offerte.png` ×6 and
  `aktions-update_erhalten.jpg` are `image-missing` **now**. The 7-work-finding cost is a
  pre-101 measurement and does not move when 101 lands — 101 touches `deal`, `sale` and
  `actie`, none of which these two keys contain.
- **Depends on 101 landing**: the claim that all six stores agree on the `actie-updates`
  block; §4(c)'s dissolution of the asymmetry; and the post-101 column of §3. If 101 is
  abandoned, "add none" still stands on reasons 1, 2 and 4 — but the asymmetry then
  survives, and the correct fix for it remains 101's (drop the `actie` arm), **not** this
