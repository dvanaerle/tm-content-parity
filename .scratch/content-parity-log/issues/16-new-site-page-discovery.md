# 16 — Discovering non-NL pages that only exist on the new site

Type: grilling
Status: resolved 2026-08-11 — premise retired: production is the source of truth

## Question

How does the log find a page that exists on the new site in a non-NL store, and
nowhere on production?

## Why this is now a sharp question

Ticket 04 built the seed list from the production sitemap and closed one half of
this. On the production side there is nothing to find: all 446 non-NL sitemap urls
cluster onto an NL page, so no page exists in a non-NL store without an NL
counterpart. hreflang missed nothing.

The new-site side stayed open, and 04 removed the cheap way to close it:

- **The new site serves no sitemap.** `/sitemap.xml`, `/sitemap/sitemap.xml` and
  `/pub/sitemap.xml` all 404 on all five hosts.
- The NL baseline found **48 pages** this way — by crawling the new site, not by
  reading the sitemap. That is 27% of the NL list, so the gap is not small.
- No store's home page is in the production sitemap either, which is a hint that
  the sitemap is a weaker seed than it looks.

For the other five stores there is no equivalent crawl and no seed for one.

## What to settle

- Is a per-store link crawl of the new site worth its cost, or does the NL crawl
  plus hreflang cover enough?
- What the crawl seeds from, given that no store home page is in the production
  sitemap either.

**Settled by ticket 11, no longer open here.** A page that exists only in a non-NL
store **is** a finding: class `orphan-page`, shown, raised against the **NL**
store, not against the store that holds it. This ticket keeps the hard half, which
is discovery: the new site serves no sitemap, so a page that nothing links to is a
crawl problem, not a rules problem.

Ticket 04 found no orphan in the production data — hreflang clustering resolved all
446 non-NL pages onto an NL key, with zero uses of the store-scoped fallback key.
The rule therefore has no known instances yet. That is a reason to measure, not a
reason to close.

## Answer

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

**Closed 2026-08-11 by a decision, not by a measurement. Production is the source
of truth, and the new site is expected to match it.**

That retires the question. This ticket asks how the log finds a page that only
the new site has, and it is worth asking only if the new site is allowed content
of its own. It is not. So the production sitemap **is** the seed by definition,
ticket [55](55-five-stores-show-all-their-pages.md) already put every production
page of every store into that store's view, and no crawl is owed.

`orphan-page` therefore never gets a producer. It was never a defect class — it
was a migration-completeness worry, and the worry is answered by the direction of
the comparison. Ticket [39](39-class-vocabulary-axes.md) item 2 already said
"declare the class, build no producer"; the reason is now stronger than "no known
instances".

### The measurement taken before the decision

Two of this ticket's premises were wrong, and both are recorded here so a re-open
starts from facts.

**"The new site serves no sitemap" was false, and it does not help.** The 2026-08-06
probe tried `/sitemap.xml`, `/sitemap/sitemap.xml` and `/pub/sitemap.xml` and did
not read `robots.txt`, which declares
`/media/siteindex/<locale>/siteindex.xml` → `media/sitemap/<locale>/{category,cms,product}.xml`.
All six stores serve one; the `.be` host serves two, `be_be` and `fr_be`, which
splits `be` from `be_fr` at the source. Production serves none of these paths —
`/media/siteindex/nl_nl/siteindex.xml` 404s there and `/media/sitemap/nl_nl/cms.xml`
answers 200 with a **PNG** — so these files are the new site's own, generated once
and stamped `2025-01-06`, with content from 2024.

It is not a usable seed and was **rejected as a source on 2026-08-11**. Measured:
`cms.xml` + `category.xml` over six stores gives 605 urls, 570 of them already in
the seed list; of the 35 it adds, **21 are already 404 on the new site too**. A
file that is 60% dead on its own novel half, with nothing added since 2024, cannot
find a page made this year.

**"No page exists in a non-NL store without an NL counterpart" was false** — 283
clusters have no NL member, per ticket [50](50-content-page-discriminator.md) — and
ticket [53](53-every-content-page-in-the-seed-list.md) settled the shape: a page
with no `nl-NL` alternate is a row of its own store, not a page NL lacks.

### What was found, and where it goes

14 urls are live on the new side and 404 on production. They are 2024 pages the
clone kept and production has since dropped — deletion candidates on the new site,
not content to migrate. **Handed to
[20](.out-of-scope/20-one-sided-pages-checklist.md)**, which owns one-sided pages, so they do not
sit inside a closed ticket. Ticket
[84](.out-of-scope/84-a-one-sided-page-carries-a-migration-decision.md) is where a row like this
records its decision.

A fuller sweep exists if it is ever wanted, and needs **no new requests**:
`data/extract/<store>/<page>.json` `.new.links[].url` holds 11,708 new-side link
records, of which 2,270 distinct internal targets are in no seed row, 2,188 of
those already measured 200-with-no-hops in `data/link-status.json`, narrowing to
about 238 plausible content pages once assets, Magento app routes and
`isProductPage()` are stripped. Two limits on it: none of that data is committed
(`.gitignore` keeps `data/*` bar four files), and `contentRoot()` scopes extraction
to `<main>`, so **header, nav and footer anchors were never captured** — a one-hop
nav harvest of six home pages is the missing piece.

**Re-open this ticket if** a new-site page turns up that production never had and
that somebody wants to keep. That is the only fact that would restore the premise.
